A protrusion can keep moving after a stimulus because its regulator remains accessible or because its actin network relaxes slowly. In two models, these alternatives produce the same traces of mixed fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET), actin and deformation. To distinguish them in microglia, pair a reporter of N-WASP accessibility with actin imaging in the same protrusion. Calibrate the reporter’s gain, sensitivity to actin and response time before choosing a sampling interval that resolves the predicted amplitude difference. In a separate feedback model, numerical results support autonomous chaos as a source of irregular displacement under constant conditions. Testing that explanation in cells calls for a separate perturbation of feedback.

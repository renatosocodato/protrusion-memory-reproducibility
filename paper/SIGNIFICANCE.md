A protrusion can respond differently to the same stimulus depending on its recent history. We show how two models can assign that history to different cellular processes, protein conformational accessibility or actin assembly, while predicting identical fluorescence, actin and deformation trajectories. We derive how an independently calibrated reporter can distinguish the two and quantify how the reporter response time, actin sensitivity and sampling interval affect the distinction. The analysis gives a way to design experiments that locate the origin of a persistent protrusion response.

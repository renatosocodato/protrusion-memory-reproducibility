# Next-frame protrusion forecast with LifeAct

The original [H1299 data](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0135204.s001) pair per-frame whole-cell RubyRed-LifeAct intensity with newly protruded pixels and include acquisition date, cell trace and original frame index. The [study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0135204) reports 122 cells in 19 independent experimental repeats. Use the original date as the preparation group, and reserve the latest seven dates. The 12 earlier dates supply all estimates. The metadata and outcome definition were examined before selecting this split. This is a retrospective transfer test.

The target is the author's Protrusion Pixels field, scaled to area at 0.21 µm/pixel, at the frame 5 min after each forecast origin. It is an area of new mask occupancy in the lab frame over one 5 min interval. Use only the first six consecutive valid samples in each cell to anchor its stationary motion level and its mean log LifeAct intensity. Begin forecasting at least one frame after this prefix. Use motion at frame t−1 and LifeAct at t. This extra motion lag ensures that even a three-frame classification of transient mask regions uses no image later than t. Skip origins spanning an index gap. Both input channels must be valid at their selected frames, and the target must be recorded. The actin input is the current log standardized whole-cell mean intensity minus its prefix mean. A constant repeat-wise multiplicative intensity scaling cancels in that contrast. No inferred trend, normalization or segment alignment uses the future frames. The processed data inherit the original image-analysis decisions. The target timestamp is 5 min after the origin. Distinguishing transient areas in the source may require retrospective adjudication after that timestamp.

Compare a constant prefix mean, delayed persistence, a fitted motion-history model, and the same history model with the LifeAct term. The motion-history term is y(t−1) minus the prefix mean. Fit the coefficients by least squares with equal weights over preparations, cells within a preparation, and eligible origins within each cell. There is no fitted intercept after baseline subtraction. Freeze all estimates before opening the held-out outcome CSV. Compare RMSE with paired preparation-specific changes and a leave-one-preparation-out range. The primary comparisons are the actin model against the stationary and the fitted motion-only models, both reported even if they disagree. The benchmark assesses short-horizon observational prediction, with no randomized change of actin abundance or identified causal mechanism.

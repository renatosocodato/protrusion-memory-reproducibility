# Independent signed-mode challenge

The v1.8 mode spectrum separated pathways at some phases only under a shared gain. This motivates a different discriminator. Hold the upstream drive fixed and change actin directly in one angular mode. For mode 2, a quarter-turn reverses its projection on a fixed cosine axis. Cross both orientations with active intervention and matched inactive delivery. The registration, projected mode, window 10–30 s and axis are fixed before examining reserved outcomes.

Use eight synthetic angular calibration preparations and 12 separate synthetic reserved preparations. Calibrate one 2×2 gain per channel from known, two-axis standards. Vary the calibration matrix between preparations. Latent shape templates come from the linear periodic ring, with a five-second actin intervention, a common fixed upstream drive and the declared 1-s acquisition with finite exposure, delay and actin reporter time. Compare mediated response, direct-only response with a successful actin first stage, control-limited delivery leakage, upstream mismatch, failed first stage and hidden leakage beyond the supplied bound. The last case deliberately violates a design assumption to test the interpretation boundary.

Aggregate signed active-minus-delivery differences within preparations. Let the predeclared meaningful displacement be 0.08 µm, minimum signed actin response 0.15 normalized units and maximum observed residual drive 0.075 units. The assigned upper drive-to-shape susceptibility is 0.55 µm per drive unit. The delivery bound is 0.025 µm except in the declared leakage case (0.16 µm). The bootstrap resamples calibration and reserved preparations independently, with 1,500 draws. Each scenario has untouched random streams, and the contrasts and decision rules are fixed. These assigned bound values and acquisition settings are for the synthetic test only.

For the repeated challenge, generate 100 new calibration/reserve pairs per case with a separately fixed root seed. Compare rates of the same predeclared decisions, with Wilson intervals for finite Monte Carlo counts. The hidden-leakage case is expected to fail under a wrong bound. It tests the consequences of an unmeasured cause, rather than the rate of false positives under a valid design.

# Independent control of mechanical leakage in mode 2

[The mode-test workflow](../../MODE_INTERVENTION.md#measure-the-mechanical-effect-in-a-third-cohort) uses angular standards, actin-disabled mechanical controls and reserved actin-intact tests from different preparations. The estimand is the signed mean contribution to one contour mode over a fixed window.

## Ring experiment

The inherited periodic ring has fixed upstream drive and a five-second actin perturbation, sampled at 1 s with 0.7-s exposure and 0.6-s delay. Mode 2 is summarized over [10, 30) s. Each synthetic experiment has 8 angular, 10 mechanical and 12 reserved preparations. Repeat each pathway/control combination on 60 independently generated cohorts. The decision uses a meaningful effect of 0.08 µm, a transfer margin of 0.020 µm, and the thresholds generated by the example plan. These are assigned test values, not GUV or cell calibrations. The report records whether the leakage-expanded interval contains the assigned actin contribution.

The transferred 0.24-µm mechanical response reproduced the raw signature of an actin-mediated response. Calibrating it produced 59 unresolved cohorts and one unqualified control, with zero excess-deformation calls. All 60 mediated cohorts cleared the bound. Direct-only cohorts produced zero calls beyond the allowance. All 60 deliberately incomplete blocks were declined. Omission of the 0.24-µm mechanical effect from the blocked state produced 58 false calls in 60 cohorts, with 2 controls unqualified. This scenario deliberately violates the specified transfer margin.

## Decision test

A separate scalar surrogate retains the additive contrast and constructs exact observational twins with different decompositions. In each split, 1,200 easy single cases and 1,200 matched twin pairs form 2,400 independent families. The development and two evaluation domains use separate fixed seeds. Some cases have an independently supplied coarse pre-acquisition bound. An illustrative selective policy learns whether acquiring an additional mechanical control would resolve a development case correctly. The analytic comparator resolves cases from the initial interval where possible, then acquires the control only for unresolved cases. After acquisition, both policies use the same signed control subtraction with a tolerance. The ring workflow instead uses a symmetric leakage allowance. The two calculations serve different design questions.

For the transferable domain, at an assigned unit cost per acquired mechanical control, the analytic rule used 0.709 controls per case with full coverage and no errors, compared with 1.000 for universal acquisition. The learned rule used 0.667 with 95.8% coverage and no errors among calls. Thus the test provides no learning advantage at matched full coverage. A passive logistic model trained with 95% mediated prevalence among reweighted ambiguous cases was wrong on all direct twins in the balanced test. In the nontransfer domain, a deliberately idealized independent mechanical witness flags both twin classes. Deferring on those classes reduced overall coverage to 33.3% for the analytic policy with zero wrong calls. Without that witness, the entire direct twin class was misattributed. The simulation assigns the witness and omits its cost. These are design trade-offs under stated generators, without guarantees for other domains or real experimental costs.

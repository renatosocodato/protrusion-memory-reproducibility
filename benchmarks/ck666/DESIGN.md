# Transfer of the CK666 velocity effect across acquisition series

Use the two independent experiments in Dataset 1 of [the cellmig study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014472) for calibration and Dataset 2 (the independent application screen) intact for scoring. CK666 at 40 µM is a shared positive control chosen for shared dose and availability, with its direction already reported by the authors. This is retrospective transfer, with no claim of a blinded biological prediction.

Within each plate, take the arithmetic mean of the published ASPC1 cell speeds per accepted well. Use finite nonnegative speeds including zeros, and require each well mean to be positive before taking its natural logarithm. Average the DMSO log means to form the plate baseline, and subtract it from the mean of CK666 log well means. Both candidates receive this same plate-matched vehicle information. Only CK666 plate endpoints are fit or scored. A missing control or treatment on any expected plate is an error.

Give the two calibration experiments equal weight, with equal plate weights within each. The stationary prediction is zero log ratio. The modulated candidate adds a single fixed log-speed shift when the known CK666 condition is active. The observation is a single aggregate from the tracked movie, with a nominal software clock and no kinetic parameter. Fit the shift with bounds [−3, 3], using Dataset 1 only, and serialize it before scoring Dataset 2.

The publication treats plates as biological replicates. In Dataset 2, conservatively combine plates with matching g1 replicate stems (.1 and .2) into one group. The remaining plate identifiers define their own groups. Compute the paired RMSEs over those held-out groups with equal groups and equal plates within group. For this same comparison report the preparation-level squared-error differences and the leave-one-group-out range of the RMSE difference as descriptive stability. No observations from Dataset 2 are used to tune parameters, scaling or choices. The evaluation concerns aggregate ASPC1 migration at one dose, with no edge-dynamics or molecular mechanism inference.
